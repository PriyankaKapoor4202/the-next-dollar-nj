# Memo: Where a children's mental health grant would reach the most New Jersey kids

**To:** Foundation program officers and public funders, children's mental health
**From:** Priyanka Kapoor, Data Analyst
**Date:** September 2026
**Dashboard:** [the-next-dollar-nj.streamlit.app](https://the-next-dollar-nj.streamlit.app/)

## Bottom line

New Jersey's children in poverty are concentrated in communities with the least nonprofit mental health capacity within reach. A $500,000 grant placed in the right community can bring up to about 930 children to the statewide typical level of access. Placed differently, the same grant reaches as few as 190.

## What the data shows

Two-thirds of the children in poverty studied (68%) live in the poorest fifth of New Jersey ZIP areas. Those communities have 37% fewer mental health and developmental nonprofits within 10 miles per child in poverty than the wealthiest fifth.

Federal shortage designations miss much of this. Of the 15 high-gap areas with the most children in poverty, 11 have no federal mental health shortage designation, including Jersey City, Union City, West New York, New Brunswick, and Atlantic City. These communities don't receive the provider incentives that designation brings.

The gap takes two forms. In dense cities like New Brunswick and Perth Amboy, services exist but are stretched across many children: about $240 to $275 of nonprofit capacity per child in poverty, against a statewide typical level near $2,580. In rural Cape May County, several communities have no relevant nonprofit within 10 miles at all.

## What a grant could do

Closing the gap in every eligible area would cost about $122 million, so targeting matters. With $500,000:

- Funding by most children reached per dollar sends the grant to Newark ZIP 07103 and brings 931 children to the typical level.
- Funding the largest community first sends it to Jersey City ZIP 07305 and brings 289 children to that level.
- Funding the deepest gap first sends it to Villas in Cape May County and brings 193 children to that level, with the largest gain per child.

About $2.3 million would close the gap entirely for Newark 07103's 4,226 children in poverty.

## Recommendation

Concentrate a grant of this size in one community rather than spreading it across many, where it would barely move any single area. Newark 07103 offers the widest reach per dollar. If the priority is the worst-off places, Cape May County is the case, where extending an existing regional provider through a satellite office, school-based services, or telehealth is likely more practical than a new organization. Hudson County and New Brunswick merit attention from state funders because they carry large gaps without federal designation.

## What to keep in mind

This analysis uses public IRS, Census, and HRSA data. It sees nonprofit headquarters but not satellite offices, and uses revenue as a rough stand-in for service capacity. It should start conversations with local providers and families, not replace them. Full method and limitations are in the project README.
